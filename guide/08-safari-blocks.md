# 8. Safari blocks

None of the rest matters if your Pokémon cannot appear. Rare Safari Zone species are gated
behind **blocks** — decorative objects you place in an area — and they only show up once you
have enough of the right kind.

To place blocks, you first unlock them through challenges with Baoba. He'll give you a handful at the beginning, and then more after every few in-game hours. To place them, just walk up to an empty space in the safari zone and click A. 

## How the gate works

Each area has a **score** in four categories, from the blocks placed in it:

`plains` · `forest` · `peak` · `water`

Every block contributes to one category. A species in an area has a requirement like
*"peak ≥ 56"*, and until the area's peak score reaches 56, that species is simply not in the
encounter table.

**Metang in Mountain needs a peak score of 56.**

Clayton knows these requirements. Set your Pokémon and area on the expedition and it shows the requirement next to the block fields, and tells you if what you've entered falls short.

It can be noted that sometimes you can change other blocks to change which encounter slot a pokemon you are targeting falls into, changing what pokemon you can get shiny from the same seed.

## How much is each block worth.

Each area can only have 30 blocks, but many pokemon require more than 30 total score for them to appear. This is because each areas' blocks are worth different numbers of points.

Each block contributes up to 7 points in its score, depending ONLY on how long **that specific area** has been active for. This day count does not reset when the safari area is removed, but does not increase when the safari area is not active, and it does not count time before the blocks were unlocked in the game. **For this reason, you'll want to unlock the blocks in Safari Zone ASAP**. Unlocking the blocks requires completing challenges from Baoba, which take **in-game time** before they are offered, and the last one requires you having the national dex.

You can check [bulbapedia](https://bulbapedia.bulbagarden.net/wiki/Johto_Safari_Zone#Area_upgrades) for details. The area upgrades chart is a little confusing, but it shows how many days it takes for an area to be active for each block type to reach 2-7 points (After 10 days plains blocks are worth 2 points, after 40 days waterside blocks are worth 2, after 100 days plains blocks are worth 4, after 250 days all blocks in that area are worth 7, etc.)

For example, let's say you want to catch Metang. It requires 56 "Peak" type block points in the Mountain area. With 30 blocks this is impossible at the beginning. After keeping the mountain area active for 30 days though, all the peak blocks will suddenly be worth 2 points each. Just place 28 peak-type objects in the mountain area, and Metang should start appearing. These do not have to be placed before the time elapses - Once the mountain has reached 30 days active, all peak blocks will be worth worth 2 points (or more if more time elapses). Nothing you can do will reduce the effectiveness of any peak blocks in that area short of "New Game". Consequently if the "Rocky Beach" are was NOT active for that time, peak blocks will still only be worth 1 point in that area.

### The date-skipping shortcuts

There are well-known tricks for advancing the Safari Zone's day counter without waiting —
changing the DS system clock forward, entering and leaving, and repeating. Community guides
describe several variants.

**They do not work reliably on every console.** Behaviour differs between DS models, between
original cartridges and flashcarts, and with emulators. Some setups reject the clock change,
some only count one day per real day regardless, some behave differently after a date change
has already been detected.

So: **try them, but verify.** After a session of skipping, check the area's actual score
before assuming it worked. Make sure you can actually encounter the pokemon you're looking for. If your console is one of the ones where it doesn't, the only reliable path is to place the blocks and genuinely wait, saving 24 hours after the last time you messed with the time.

This is also an argument for setting blocks up **early** — well before you plan to hunt.
