# 7. Safari blocks

None of the rest matters if your Pokémon cannot appear. Rare Safari Zone species are gated
behind **blocks** — decorative objects you place in an area — and they only show up once you
have enough of the right kind.

## How the gate works

Each area has a **score** in four categories, from the blocks placed in it:

`plains` · `forest` · `peak` · `water`

Every block contributes to one category. A species in an area has a requirement like
*"peak ≥ 56"*, and until the area's peak score reaches 56, that species is simply not in the
encounter table.

**Metang in Mountain needs a peak score of 56.**

Clayton knows these requirements. Set your Pokémon and area on the expedition and it shows
the requirement next to the block fields, and tells you if what you've entered falls short.

> **Screenshot:** the block-score fields with the requirement shown.

## Getting the numbers

Clayton wants the **same numbers PokeFinder takes** — day-multiplier applied.

Easiest route: open the area in PokeFinder's Safari Zone tooling and copy the four scores
across. If you're counting by hand, count the blocks placed in the area and apply the
multiplier for how many days they've been there.

> **Get this right before a long hunt.** A wrong block score does not produce an error — it
> produces an encounter table that silently doesn't contain your Pokémon, and you find out
> after the run. If Safari Compass's in-house frame search reports *"no metang frame in
> range"*, suspect your blocks first.

## Placing blocks, and the waiting

The ordinary way is slow. Blocks take **in-game days** to take effect, and their contribution
grows over roughly ten days. Reaching a score of 56 by placing blocks and waiting is a
multi-week proposition in real time.

### The date-skipping shortcuts

There are well-known tricks for advancing the Safari Zone's day counter without waiting —
changing the DS system clock forward, entering and leaving, and repeating. Community guides
describe several variants.

**They do not work reliably on every console.** Behaviour differs between DS models, between
original cartridges and flashcarts, and with emulators. Some setups reject the clock change,
some only count one day per real day regardless, some behave differently after a date change
has already been detected.

So: **try them, but verify.** After a session of skipping, check the area's actual score
before assuming it worked. If your console is one of the ones where it doesn't, the only
reliable path is to place the blocks and genuinely wait.

This is also an argument for setting blocks up **early** — well before you plan to hunt.

## Time of day

Encounter tables also vary by in-game time. Clayton uses three buckets:

| Bucket | In-game clock |
|---|---|
| morning | 04:00 – 09:59 |
| day | 10:00 – 19:59 |
| night | 20:00 – 03:59 |

Every hour falls in exactly one. A target's initial time therefore fixes which table applies —
worth checking that your species actually appears in that bucket before committing to a hunt
at that hour.

## A checklist before hunting

- [ ] Right area for the species
- [ ] Blocks placed, and enough days elapsed for them to count
- [ ] Score meets the requirement Clayton shows
- [ ] Scores entered into the expedition, day-multiplier applied
- [ ] Species appears at your target's time of day
- [ ] Safari Compass's frame search finds frames without complaining
